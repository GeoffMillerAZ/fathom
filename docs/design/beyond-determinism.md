# Beyond determinism: winning where deterministic backing does not win today

Status: Proposed 2026-11-12 (architect ruling, owner review pending). Records: ADR-105, ADR-106, ADR-107 and invariants I76, I77 (proposed). Vision: `docs/vision/46-proof-at-the-moment-of-change.md`. Siblings: `docs/design/library-roadmap.md` (the library side of every target), `docs/design/change-hazards.md` (HZ- hazard rows, the hazard engine), `docs/assessments/2026-11-12-library-uses.md` (the census of library uses and the capability tables).

Verdict: the paths 2 and 3 that hold are the ones that end in a deterministic test or join. Three moves matter most: compile one rule from one incident, test the thesis on ground fathom does not own, and let outside oracles grade fathom before they feed it. The last section lists them.

Codes in this document: BY-F finding, BY-L law, BY-FW flywheel loop, BY-D decision, BY-R risk. Targets keep their original ids: V-N1 to V-N10 from vision 46 and A-N1 to A-N6 for the policy review's N1 to N6. V-N10 has two halves: rule breadth is A-N1 and value resolution is A-N2. Path numbers follow the ladder: 1 make it deterministic, 2 hybrid, 3 pair, 4 refuse with a wins-if and a date. Marks: NOW, ENTRY (scheduled behind a condition), FUTURE (a register entry with a trigger), REFUSE. T-IaC is the recurring trigger: a fleet repository with Terraform or CloudFormation, or the policy tool importing the library, or an outside adopter named in writing. OQ3 and OQ15 are the two owner questions this design leans on. Hazard rows use the HZ- prefix of `docs/plans/change-hazards.md`. Other row ids (CHG-, FCT-, BEN-, UND-, REC-, INB-, LIB-, RL-, CAP-) live in their plans and in the library roadmap. `[prior]` is unverified this round.

## 1. What the grounding found

- BY-F1 Every measured model judgment inside fathom missed its bar. Routing was right 45.7% of the time at a stated confidence of 0.9 or more, though the same site showed a small paired gain in an A/B (+0.047). Reranking read a paired mean of -0.19. On four computed code templates a fast model read below the lexical baseline on three and tied on one. On five real questions the model table was right once, at 1.39 times the context. Sources: `docs/bench/readings/decision-port-calibration-2026-09-24.md`, `decision-port-ab-2026-09-23.md`, and `docs/bench/readings/decide/`.
- BY-F2 The one model use that works is batch authoring behind a deterministic gate. The layer compiler cost $27.50 for 4,025 claims. Its gate rejected 56% of what the model proposed. 97% of drift healed with no model. Its weak points are selection and effect (4 violations against 4), not construction.
- BY-F3 Pairing is half built and unread. Six foreign coverage formats parse and have no reading (TQ-M3). `review_diff` takes Go profiles only. `mutation-killed` tops the strength ladder and nothing corroborates it at ingest. SCIP import is complete, the last live count was zero SCIP-tier edges (2026-09-27, not re-measured), and init runs two of thirteen mapped indexers, only when they are already installed. Findings import held zero outside findings at that count, and no scanner reader exists.
- BY-F4 Prose to check is a hole, and the hole is vocabulary. There are four guard check kinds, which take no arguments beyond scopes, and five layer rule kinds. Seven rules are enforced in this repository. Deterministic proposers exist and end at a person editing a file: `intent adr-rules`, `intent export --guard`, `findings candidates`.
- BY-F5 Two measurements are open in public `[prior]`. No published number says how accurate a model-drafted rule is. No independent accuracy result exists for local meaning-search servers.
- BY-F6 A plan already separates shape from value. Its JSON carries actions, replace paths and action reasons beside boolean maps of what is unknown and what is sensitive. No tool found emits the value-free part alone. Replace paths carry map keys and set elements, so a summary must hash them.
- BY-F7 Whether the fleet holds infrastructure code is unverified. This repository holds none outside test data, and the policy tool is unbuilt. A census of the fleet comes first (OQ15 asks the owner the same question). The code side has consumers today either way.
- BY-F8 The acceptance rule of BY-L4 has no working instance. `rules replay` runs three layer rule kinds over a Go import graph. It replays no guard check. This repository's own 200-commit replay caught nothing, so history holds no known-bad commit for those rules. No mutant deriver exists for rule kinds.

## 2. Laws for every win path

- BY-L1 **Targets, not refusals.** Each item on the two lists gets a path, a measurement, and either a scheduled row or a trigger. A refusal needs a reason, a wins-if and a date.
- BY-L2 **Weakest evidence class wins.** A paired or hybrid source can lower the evidence class of an answer and never raise it. Three classes are added: `declared context` (a declared or placed baseline, or a list a person keeps), `attested` (a producer, a commit and an age) and `accepted draft` (a drafted check with its receipt).
- BY-L3 **A model never resolves an unknown, writes a verdict, sets a status or extends a fact list at run time.** It may draft, rank inside a tier, or phrase a cited set. This is I62 and ADR-056 rule R7, kept.
- BY-L4 **The acceptance rule for drafts.** A draft is never served. Three things must hold before use. Fidelity: it agrees with labels fathom did not derive and the drafter did not write. Those are a public labelled set, the commit that caused an incident, or examples a person confirmed, with a stated minimum count. Replay over history is not a label. It only measures noise. Strength: every mutant derived from the check is killed with the predicted transition. Strength alone proves that each clause matters, and a misread sentence kills its own mutants. Acceptance: a person accepts the rule and writes it into the repository. Fathom never writes that file. The test is a precondition for the person's decision, never a replacement. A draft with no outside label is advice and cannot gate, and so is a draft whose kind has no mutant deriver yet. The receipt binds the draft, label and catalog digests and reads stale when one moves. The library roadmap reaches the same rule (its draft acceptance test, LIB-10, and RL-8).
- BY-L5 **Two drafters, no judge.** At run time the caller is the model: fathom scaffolds the agent that is already there with a cited set, the unknowns and a bounded question. For batch authoring the drafter is the layer compiler's pipeline, which exists, is pinned and priced, and already rejects most of what its model proposes (BY-F2). Both end at BY-L4. The decision port gains no kind and no judged site.
- BY-L6 **Oracle before input.** An outside source grades fathom on the bench before the product ingests it. A plan grades plan-free hazards. A SCIP index grades call extraction. Runtime edges grade static links.
- BY-L7 **No new store and no fetch.** A paired input is a file the operator placed, with a producer, a commit and a time. It enters through an envelope that exists (attested test results, the review kit outbox, catalogs) or is read at request time. This is I56 and ADR-051 decision 1, kept.
- BY-L8 **Win first where a consumer exists.** A path with no consumer today is a register entry with a trigger, not a roadmap row (the admission test's clause 5, applied to old rows and new ones alike).

## 7. Risks

- BY-R1 Acceptance passes a check that is wrong in a way no label covers. Mitigation: a check with no outside label cannot gate, a person decides, and adjudicated disagreements become labels.
- BY-R2 Pairing turns fathom into a format zoo. Mitigation: BY-L6 to BY-L8. Each reader names its consumer before it is built, and the first step is reading what is already parsed.
- BY-R3 "Scaffold the caller" cannot be told apart from the harness, and the caller's drafts are not pinned or priced. Mitigation: paired arms with pins on model, harness and tool schema, and the compiler route for anything that must be repeatable.
- BY-R4 Fifteen targets dilute the core. Mitigation, with the new rows listed: readings that start now (TQ-M3, the firing ranking, the cold-latency reading, the census), and new rows CHG-8, CHG-9, CHG-10, CHG-11, CHG-12, FCT-11, BEN-10, BEN-11, LIB-7, LIB-9, LIB-11 to LIB-14 and RL-2 to RL-5, with RL-1, RL-6, RL-8 and RL-9 on rows that exist. The rest wait in the register.
- BY-R5 The register hides a refusal behind a trigger nobody pulls. Mitigation: OQ3 and OQ15 are read on 2027-01-31 with OQ2.
- BY-R6 The acceptance rule never gets an instance, because incidents with a clear causing commit are rare. Then V-N4 stays on path 1 and the holdouts, and that is recorded on 2027-01-31.
- BY-R7 A whole-program lifetime check loses to the linters (RL-3). Stated in its own kill.

## 8. Decision models

This design adds no kind and no judged site to the decision port (BY-D2, BY-L5).

- Typed questions it could ask: none at run time. A model never resolves an unknown, writes a verdict, sets a status or extends a fact list (BY-L3). The only model uses are drafting a rule from an incident (RL-8) and batch authoring through the layer compiler, and both end at the acceptance rule of BY-L4, never at a served answer.
- Deterministic fallback with provider `none`: complete. Every path in section 3 has a deterministic arm, and a draft that is never produced leaves the rule as advice or absent.
- Opt-out: no drafting runs unless the caller or the compiler is invoked. Accepted rules are files a person wrote into the repository, so removing a model changes nothing already accepted.
- Measurement that proves or kills each: the model arms of BEN-10 and BEN-11, the pack arm of V-N7, and held-out accuracy of accepted drafts against fixture-tested rules (CHG-10). Each can lose and is published either way. J0 reruns once per model generation.

## 9. The three moves that matter most

1. **Compile once, starting from one incident (BY-L4, RL-8).** The hybrid at its best is a check drafted once and proven forever. Nothing proves a draft today (BY-F8). The first instance is a rule drafted from a real incident, accepted when it fires on the commit that caused it, stays quiet over history and a person signs it. It needs one incident, replay widened to that rule's kind (CHG-11) and a mutant deriver for it (CHG-12). Until those exist this is a plan, not a result, and a rule accepted without them is advice.
2. **Test the thesis on ground fathom does not own.** The public hazard benchmark with scanner and model arms is the cleanest such test, because a plan is the oracle. The reading benchmark is the second, once its questions are ones an agent needs and its oracles are not fathom's own. The meaning-search arm on conceptual questions and the review pack arm follow. Each can lose, and each is published either way.
3. **Oracle before input (BY-L6), starting with what is already built and already live.** Read HZ-1, because its finding is served to agents today with its floor unread. Read TQ-M3, because six coverage formats parse and nobody has checked them. Both cost nothing and need no code. Then finish the pairings and let plans, coverage and indexes grade fathom before they feed it.

The size of this plan on the infrastructure side is set by who will use it. The first wave's census and one answer from the owner (OQ15) decide that.
