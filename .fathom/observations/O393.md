### O393 — the throughput gate is judged on 6 KB for Cloud Custodian: six runs of the same reading gave 0.68 to 2.01 MB/s under load 20, and only the runs above 1.0 meet L2

- **Symptom:** `level_met` "none" on a re-run of an unchanged dialect: `throughput_mb_s` .81, .87, .74, .68 against 1.17, 1.31, 1.61, 1.73 and 2.01
- **Evidence:**
  - **Tool:** `indexbench lang-oracle -dialect cloud-custodian` on cloud-custodian/cloud-custodian bf126f38 (13 files, 6,058 bytes, 7.3 ms of CPU), the host's load average near 20 from other waves
  - **Expected:** a throughput reading that does not depend on the machine's other work
  - **Actual:** the gate (`>= 1 MB/s/core` on CPU time) divides a few kilobytes by a few milliseconds; the same dialect on Azure/azure-policy (5,658 files) reads 4.7 MB/s and on camp 5.05
  - **Repro:** six consecutive runs of the committed command (the reading kept is the third attempt of a retry loop that stops at the first run whose gate passes: the first two read .74 and .68, the third 2.01)
- **Root cause:** the YAML policy files of the pinned subject are few and small (19 files, 36 policies; the project's examples are .rst code blocks)
- **Fix:** none; a larger YAML subject would stabilise it (LB-Q-cloud-custodian-rest)
- **Status:** open
- **Impact:** the reading's L2 depends on a timing gate at a size it cannot measure; the dialect's cost on larger subjects is far above the bar
