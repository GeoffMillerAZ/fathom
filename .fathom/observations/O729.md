### O729 — lang-oracle's throughput on a small corpus reads the machine's load: 0.77 to 1.60 MB/s over five readings of the same 17 KB, flipping level_met between L2 and none

- **Symptom:** the jenkinsfile reading, all metrics 1, reads `level_met` none on some runs and L2 on others: only the always-required `throughput >= 1 MB/s/core` (CPU) moves
- **Evidence:** `indexbench lang-oracle -dialect jenkinsfile` at fathom `912051b9`, five runs back to back under the runtime lock at load average 11 to 13: 1.60, 1.35, 0.85, 0.77, 1.21 MB/s; five earlier runs 1.09 to 1.55, three single-subject runs 0.69 to 1.14. The corpus is 23 files, 17 KB (per-file CPU under 1 ms). A loop extracting the same files 20 times after a warm-up reads 1.40 to 1.76 MB/s, level with the gradle dialect on the same blob (1.44 to 1.56); the 140 KB tolerated run reads 1.82 to 2.37
- **Root cause:** per-file CPU on sub-millisecond extractions is dominated by scheduling and cache effects under load; the gate sums them over too few bytes to average them out (cause by measurement, not isolated further)
- **Fix:** none here (cmd/indexbench and internal/langbench are outside this item); the committed reading is the median of the five (1.21) and the markdown states the spread. A minimum measured byte count (repeat the timed pass until N bytes) would end it
- **Status:** open
- **Impact:** a small tool-format corpus can lose or gain its level on a re-measure for no extraction change
