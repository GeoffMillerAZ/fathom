### O453 — host-side marshalling is not where a wasm query's cost is: dropping a failing match before resolving its captures saved 0.5%

- **Symptom:** the first suspect for retrofit's HTML was `collectMatches`: one guest call (`fathom_node_end_byte`) per capture of every match, 963,425 captures over 308,188 matches, 94% of the matches dropped by predicates afterwards.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Tool:** instructions retired over the 536 html pages, before and after resolving a pattern's predicate captures first and dropping a failing match before its other captures became Nodes (the matches kept byte-equal to `filterByPredicates`, tested): 50.22 G against 49.96 G (and 50.16 G against 49.77 G on a rerun).
  - **Expected:** a few percent at least, if per-capture guest calls were the cost.
  - **Actual:** 0.5%. A query of one trivial pattern (`(document) @d`, one match per file) already costs 4.1 G over the parse, and each pattern adds its structural-match work in the guest (O452); the guest, not the host, holds the cost.
- **Root cause:** none to fix; the change was reverted rather than kept as complexity for 0.5%.
- **Fix:** none (see O452 for what moved the cost).
- **Status:** closed (not a cause)
- **Impact:** the next reader of tsengine's `collectMatches` should not retry it; the wasm parse (3.2k instructions per byte of HTML, 4.7k of Kotlin) and the per-pattern query cost are what is left.
