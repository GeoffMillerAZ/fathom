### O1446 — On the fleet daemon a graph-backed call usually meets a cold graph: every ingest and every restart evicts it, and symbol_context then gives up at 60 s with nothing

- **Symptom:** `symbol_context`, `review_diff` and qualified lookups are abandoned at the tree-class deadline (`-32603 abandoned: the call's deadline passed … no partial answer is served`), most often on app-kit. The agent falls back to grep and source reads. Adopters filed 41 reports of it between 2026-09-18 and 2026-10-18, the largest group in the adopter triage (docs/bench/readings/feedback/adopter-triage-2026-10-20.md, theme T01).
- **Evidence:**
  - **Reports:** app-kit O84, O90, O104, O107, O109, O110, O117, O119, O121, O126, O131, O132 (symbol_context), O111 (review_diff), O91 (exact find_symbol), O56 (pack_context), O93 and O108 (verify claims dropped, then came back on retry); adopter repository A O13, O14, O16, O17, O21; adopter repository B O13, O15; adopter repository C O12.
  - **Call log (the fleet daemon, 2026-07-07 to 2026-10-20, agent-origin calls):** `symbol_context` abandoned 511 of 2,050 times on fathom (25%) and 50 of 178 on app-kit (28%). `review_diff` was abandoned 42 of 112 times on app-kit (38%). Since 2026-10-15, app-kit's `symbol_context` calls that succeeded took 32.5 s at p50 and 51.3 s at p90, so even the successes ran close to the 60 s deadline. Its successful `review_diff` calls took 79.5 s at p50. fathom's successful `symbol_context` calls took 249 ms at p50.
  - **Churn (daemon stderr):** daemon starts per day: 9 on 10-16, 3 on 10-17, 307 on 10-18 (the O1233 restart loop), 14 on 10-20 by 10:20Z. app-kit auto-refresh requests on drift: 41 on 10-16, 80 on 10-17, 57 on 10-18.
  - **Probe, 2026-10-20, deployed binary 909ea5c2e:** right after the 09:56Z restart, fathom `symbol_context askRuleVerdict` was abandoned at 60 s. Minutes later it answered in 7 s, and `GraphFor` also answered in 7 s. After the 10:15Z restart, `GraphFor` was abandoned at 86 s. On app-kit, a service-qualified `symbol_context` was abandoned at 60 s twice after app-kit's 10:02Z refresh request. `find_symbol` and `blast_radius` on app-kit answered in 0 to 4 s throughout.
- **Root cause:** `memo.EvictStoreService` (internal/memo/graphstore.go) drops a service's snapshot, typed edges and graphs as soon as any ingest of it completes. A daemon restart drops everything. Only a single-file refresh patches the graph (`PatchPackage`). The graph flight is kept past half its 2 s expected cost, so a build does finish after the caller leaves. The next caller benefits only if no ingest or restart comes first. On an actively developed repository, the post-commit hook and auto-refresh on drift ingest between almost every pair of agent calls, so the agent nearly always pays a cold snapshot plus a graph build. On app-kit that takes 30 to 60 s or more, at the 60 s tree deadline. `symbol_context` loads the snapshot and its graphs (`loadNavGraphsErr`, internal/mcp/nav_tools.go) before it resolves the name. Even the definition and the ambiguity answer wait for the build, so a call abandoned at the deadline returns nothing. O1014 records the related budget cause, where the graph does not stay resident at shed. This entry records the eviction cause. It holds at the deployed 4 GiB budget.
- **Fix:** proposed, not built. (1) Within the table freeze: when the graph for the service is not resident (`memo` Peek) and the call's remaining deadline is shorter than the service's measured build (O1236's per-service cost record), `symbol_context` answers the definition from the same store lookup `find_symbol` uses, with no callers or tests, and a field that says the graph is still building. `review_diff` serves its non-graph sections the same way. (2) After RT-M1 (cache freeze): patch the graph on a full ingest from the changed packages instead of evicting it. That change adds no new cache, but it changes eviction policy, so it is RT territory.
- **Status:** fixed (90700acc0, f6db5e73b), the partial-answer half for `symbol_context`. When the graph is not resident and its measured build (`memo.NavBuildCost`, O1236's per-service record) does not fit the call's deadline less a reserve, `symbol_context` warms the build one stage at a time (snapshot, syntax graph, typed edges, both, resolved), each held `memo.NavKeepAfter` (1 s) plus 100 ms so `Group.leave` keeps the flight, and stops at the first stage still building. It answers `def` (signature included) and `index_commit` from the resident snapshot or, when that is cold too, from the store lookup `find_symbol`'s exact tier uses, `partial: "call graph not built (cold): …"`, and `callers`/`callees`/`related_tests` as `"not computed (cold graph)"`. A snippet is served only from a resident snapshot, which can check the file for secret-valued definitions. When the build fits, the call waits up to the deadline less the reserve and answers the same partial if it still has not finished. Test: `TestSymbolContextColdGraphAnswersPartial`. Fixed (c05a8751e), the name half: the name resolves before any wait on the graph, against the resident snapshot or, when that is cold, the store lookup `find_symbol` uses. A name with more than one definition answers the ambiguity and a name with none answers `not_found` at once; a store miss is checked against the snapshot within one `memo.NavKeepAfter` hold, so the nearest names are listed when it loads in time. Only a unique name waits for the graph. A service with no build measured since the server started (`memo.NavBuildMeasured`) is taken as a build longer than the deadline: the call warms the stages and answers the partial, which says the build time is unknown. Tests: `TestSymbolContextAnswersAmbiguousNameWithoutTheGraph`, `TestSymbolContextUnknownBuildCostAnswersPartialAtOnce`.
- **Answers:** app-kit O84, O90, O104, O107, O109, O110, O117, O119, O121, O126, O131, O132; adopter repository A O13, O14, O16, O17, O21; adopter repository B O13, O15; adopter repository C O12 (the `symbol_context` reports). Not answered here: app-kit O111 (`review_diff`), O91, O56, O93 and O108.
- **Measured:** before, on the deployed daemon (2026-10-20 11:40Z): app-kit `symbol_context main` abandoned at 60.0 s twice in a row. After, on a scratch serve-http of f6db5e73b over a scratch store holding only app-kit (4 GiB budget, port not the daemon's), each call right after a restart with a 3 s deadline: 1.42, 1.17, 1.32, 1.17 s partial answers while the stages warmed, then 0.51 s full and 0.12 s warm; the origin/main binary on the same store was abandoned at 3.03 s twice. With the default 60 s deadline the scratch build fits (4.2 s cold, full answer), so the fleet-sized cold case is the 3 s probe's shape, not its figures. After, on the deployed daemon (21fc4b309, 2026-10-20 13:33Z, first calls after the deploy restart, the daemon shedding refreshes): app-kit `symbol_context main` answered every time and was never abandoned, at 55.08, 13.04 and 55.10 s. Each answer was the ambiguous answer (92 definitions). Those calls took the fits-the-deadline branch, because after a restart `memo.NavBuildCost` has no measured build and uses `ExpectedCost`. They waited to the deadline reserve on a build that did not finish, then answered from the resident snapshot. After the name half, on a scratch serve-http over app-kit and agentic-kit (4 GiB budget, default deadline), first call after each restart, origin/main 7794f805f against c05a8751e: `symbol_context main` 4.14 and 3.69 s against 0.05 and 0.02 s (ambiguous, 68 definitions in that clone); a unique name (`BenchmarkMaximumRowsCSV`) 4.26 s full against 1.41 s and 1.20 s partial, then 0.29 s full on the third call. Each call advances the build by at least one stage, so a unique name can answer partial more than once after a restart. Deployed figures after the deploy are the ship step's.
- **Still open:** the eviction half (2), patching the graph on a full ingest instead of evicting it, stays deferred until RT-M1 reads: it changes eviction policy. `review_diff`'s non-graph sections are not yet served the same way.
- **Impact:** the adopters' most common failure. It turns fathom's headline tool into a 60 s wait followed by a grep on the repositories that use it most.
