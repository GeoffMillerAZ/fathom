### O482 — the oracle-blind cap sums over subjects, so one big typed subject carries two untyped ones under it

- **Symptom:** with webpack in the reading the cap (10%) does not fire (899 of 26,375 fathom pairs, 3.4%) and L2 reads excellent, while axios (180 of 827, 21.8%) and express (242 of 614, 39.4%) each exceed it alone; the reading's call metrics are 96% webpack's pairs.
- **Evidence:** `recompute` in internal/langbench/merge.go (LB-F203): the cap is computed from counts summed over the reading's subjects, as the owner-architect decision (2026-10-02, ADR-048) words it. Axios + express alone: cap fired, call_precision .4365 (629 of 1,441), L1.
- **Root cause:** the owner's rule is about the reading; a reading's ratios are sums (never averages of ratios) everywhere else in the harness, so a subject's share is its pair count.
- **Fix:** disclosure, not a second rule: a note names every subject over the cap alone when the reading is under it (`oracle-blind share: ...`, tested), and the JavaScript L2 note carries each untyped subject's own precision (.8300, .2473).
- **Status:** open (owner decision: per-subject cap, or a share-weighted cap, or the sum as is; the sum's effect is that adding any large typed subject to a language raises its level without improving the grading of its untyped code)
- **Impact:** the JavaScript reading's L2 excellent is true of typed JavaScript and says little about express-style code; LB-Q-javascript-rest says so.
