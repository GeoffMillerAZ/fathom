### O489 — LB-M5's CPU ratio is load-dependent (1.50x at load 10, 1.60x at load 4); the instruction ratio is 1.60x either way

- **Symptom:** two protocol runs of the same two binaries (base 56c18502, head b1a764b2) read different CPU ratios.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Tool:** `nice -n 10 /usr/bin/time -l fathom ingest --json` over the w6 set rebuilt from the baseline readings' 17 SHAs (6,524 files, 75,971,918 bytes), fresh store, FATHOM_HOME and FATHOM_CACHE_ROOT each run, base, head, base, head.
  - **Actual:** at load 9.5 to 12.4 the second runs read base 16.95 s and head 25.50 s CPU (1.504x; instructions 237.0 G and 379.8 G, 1.60x); at load 3.5 to 5.0 base 14.67 s and head 23.47 s (1.600x; 236.5 G and 379.4 G, 1.604x). The base's CPU falls 13% with the load and the head's 8%.
  - **Wall:** 17.56 s and 25.91 s, 1.476x, under the 1.5x the row's own text names; D1 (design §1.5) takes the verdict on CPU.
- **Root cause:** the machine's load inflates the cheaper, more syscall-bound base more than the head; CPU seconds in a shared pool are not a ratio to trust at 1.5 +/- 0.1.
- **Fix:** none here. Read LB-M5 from instructions retired beside CPU, and take the CPU reading at a load under 6 on the 10 cores.
- **Status:** open (a measurement-method note)
- **Impact:** a PASS or FAIL within 1.4x to 1.6x CPU is not decided by one protocol run.
