### O487 — content sniffs ran Go's NFA over every JSON and YAML file five times over, 16 G of chinook's 22 G

- **Symptom:** chinook-database (13 SQL files and a 1.9 MB data JSON) cost 21.9 G instructions for 8.9 MB and `regexp.(*machine).match` was 0.95 s of the whole set's CPU.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Tool:** a CPU profile of the 17-repository ingest, `-peek DocExtractor.DetectsContent` and `resolveExtractor`; per-subject ingests with fathom at 90f0a01f and after the cut.
  - **Actual:** `DetectsContent` runs a dialect's `detect.content_sniff`, an unanchored multi-line alternation, over the whole source; the three cloud-policy sniffs, CloudFormation's and OpenAPI's ran on every `.json`, `.yaml` and `.yml`, and `resolveExtractor` runs in four passes (extract, doc xref, policy ref, route ref).
  - **Measured:** chinook 21.9 G to 6.0 G; the whole set 415.5 G to 396.2 G.
- **Root cause:** an NFA scan for a pattern whose every match must contain a word the source usually lacks.
- **Fix:** LB-F209: `sniffLiterals` reads the pattern's syntax tree for the literals a match must contain; `DetectsContent` answers false without the NFA when the source holds none. 14 of 14 manifests' sniffs carry one; `TestSniffPrefilterNeverChangesAnAnswer` checks it over the repository's JSON, YAML and Terraform files.
- **Status:** fixed
- **Impact:** 5% of the set's instructions; any tree with large JSON or YAML (lockfiles, data dumps, generated specs).
