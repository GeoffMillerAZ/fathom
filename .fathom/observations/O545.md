### O545 — scip-ruby on Sorbet-typed gems cannot grade the Ruby dialect's L2 as scored: constant references, `new`, operators and class-body calls are read differently

- **Symptom:** against scip-ruby 0.5.0 run with each gem's own sorbet/config (0 errors), the dialect reads L2 call_recall .8355 and call_precision .5845 on Shopify/ruby-lsp v0.26.11, and .8566 and .7511 on Shopify/spoom v1.8.10
- **Evidence:**
  - **Tool:** `indexbench lang-oracle -dialect ruby -scip <index> -unmatched` (wave lx, fathom cfa58b63, the oracle-blind rule on: 493 and 118 pairs left out)
  - **Expected:** a tier-A truth over the same call contract the dialect records
  - **Actual:** fathom-only pairs 6,844 and 1,915: constant references 4,931 and 1,544, `new` 566 and 189, the rest calls on untyped receivers written without parentheses. Oracle-only pairs 1,895 and 968: `initialize` 566 and 210, operators 1,039 and 393, class-body calls 206 and 211
  - **Repro:** calibration/oracles/scip-ruby/README.md, section "Sorbet-typed subjects"
- **Root cause:** scip-ruby references a class as `Foo#`, which scipBareName reads as not callable, while the dialect records a constant as a call (upstream tags.scm's contract). Sorbet names `Foo.new` as `Foo#initialize`, references operator methods, and the index's enclosing ranges pair a class-body call with its class. The dialect pairs a call with its method only and records no operator
- **Fix:** none here (ScoreSCIPWith is outside wave lx's files). Ruby rules in the harness would read a constant reference as a call to its name and `new` as the `initialize` the index names, and leave operators and class-body calls out of L2
- **Status:** open
- **Impact:** a typed subject cannot lift Ruby's L2 above good until the harness reads scip-ruby's vocabulary; LB-Q-ruby-rest's L2 ceiling
